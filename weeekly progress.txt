Right now Story Sprout has most of the underlying information, but it is scattered:

The story has a reading goal and curriculum objective.
The story has vocabulary words in the Word garden.
The progress endpoint stores the learner’s goals and latest activity.
The latest activity shows only a few vocabulary words.

So the product is recording learning evidence, but it is not yet presenting a useful learning record.

What “reading goal” means

The reading goal is the skill being practiced through the story. It is separate from the story’s theme.

For example:

Story theme: A child explores a moonlit garden.
Reading goal: Comprehension.
Specific objective: Answer questions using details from the story.
Vocabulary: observe, pathway, discover.
Evidence: The child answered 2 of 3 story questions correctly.
A parent-friendly explanation could say:

“This story helped Maya practice comprehension by finding important details and answering questions about what happened.”

For another story:

“This story helped Maya practice vocabulary by meeting new words in context and discussing what they mean.”

The curriculum code can remain available, but it should be secondary. Parents should see the plain-English explanation first.

What I would add

On the Home progress area, I would add a section called What your reader practiced with four parts:

Reading goal

“Comprehension”
“Finding details and explaining what happened”
Words encountered

Show all vocabulary from recent stories, not only the latest three.
Include the word, child-friendly definition, story title, and date.
Let the parent mark a word as “Practiced again” or revisit it.
What the reader showed

“Answered 2 of 3 story questions correctly”
“Used details from the story”
“Practiced perspective and evidence”
For Grades 6–8, show evidence, inference, theme, perspective, or author craft when applicable.
Recent learning history

Date
Story title
Goal
Objective
Vocabulary
Story-check result
Whether the story was completed
The wording should be careful. We should say:

Practiced
Encountered
Showed evidence of
Received positive story-check feedback
We should not say:

“Mastered reading”
“Learned permanently”
“Passed the grade”
“Reading level improved”
A single story cannot prove overall mastery. But a collection of story activities can give parents a meaningful picture of what the child is practicing and where they may need more support.

My recommendation is to make this a prominent part of the Progress area and also include a shorter version after each story:

Today’s reading work
Goal: Finding details
Words: observe, pathway, discover
Story check: 2 of 3 correct
Next step: Try another story with the same goal

That would make Story Sprout feel much more educational and trustworthy. The parent would understand not only that a story was created, but why it was created and what the child did with it.
