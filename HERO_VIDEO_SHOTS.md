# StoryAura Land Hero Video — 6-shot brief (animated 3D cartoon, stylized HD)

**Status: draft for review (2026-10-06), revision 2.** The story is created and read in the web app. A parent can print it, so they can read the paper copy together or keep reading on screen. The video shows that flow. It must **not** show shelves of books or a child with a store-bought book, because that is not the product. Everything is stylized 3D cartoon animation, with no photorealistic people, so there are no real-child or model-release issues. Edit freely before generating.

- **Shots 1–4 and 6 feature the boy** in the same stylized 3D cartoon style. The descriptions below are animation / keyframe direction and generation prompts.
- **The mom is in shots 1, 2, 5, and 6**, in the matching cartoon style, so the whole spot looks like one world.
- Total runtime: 60 seconds across 6 shots, at the highest resolution practical (4K preferred, 1080p minimum). Aspect 16:9 master; keep the key action inside a center-safe area so a 9:16 crop works for social.

Lock the character designs once, before generating, and keep them identical in every shot:

> **BOY (locked):** a **10-year-old boy**, an original stylized 3D cartoon character: short curly auburn hair, light-brown skin, big expressive eyes, a friendly gap-toothed smile, wearing a plain teal hoodie with a small star patch. His proportions should read as a 10-year-old (a bit taller and less round-faced than a preschooler), not a toddler or a teen. Keep the model sheet, proportions, and shading identical across all his shots.
>
> **MOM (locked):** an original stylized cartoon mother, shoulder-length dark wavy hair, a cream cardigan, small round earrings, warm and relaxed.
>
> **MASCOT (locked):** a small original plush fox with one floppy ear and a stitched smile, in plain orange and cream. It lives on the sofa beside the boy. It is also the hero of the boy's story, so the boy sees "his" fox in the adventure.

**Setting (locked):** one cozy living room with a sofa, a coffee table, a floor lamp, a window, and a small home printer on a shelf. The same room is used in every shot. Only the light changes, from late afternoon to warm morning.

**Copyright and originality rules (important):**
- Every character, prop, and setting is **original**. Do not imitate, reference, or prompt for any existing cartoon, movie, game, book, or studio style or character (no Pixar, Disney, Dr. Seuss, Pokémon, Minecraft, etc.), and don't use "in the style of [brand or artist]" in generation prompts.
- No real brands, logos, or trademarks anywhere: no logos on clothing, laptops, tablets, phones, printers, or toys.
- Use only music, sound effects, and voice you own, have licensed for commercial use, or generate with a tool whose terms allow commercial use. Keep the license receipts.
- Check the generation or animation tool's commercial-use terms before publishing, and keep the final character sheet on file as proof of the original design.

**No brand name, logo, or URL baked into any shot. No readable on-screen text baked into the render.** Never show a device screen face-on. Angle it away from camera or show only its soft animated glow. Story pages and the app interface appear **only through overlays** (see below). The printed pages are shown as blank or softly blurred cream sheets, with no readable text.

**Graphics are added in post, not baked into the animation.** Anything marked **[overlay: ...]** is a note for the video editor to add afterward as a clean 2D motion-graphic layer on top of the render. The closing brand card and URL are also added in post, in shot 6.

**What this video must say (ranked):**
1. A story made for *this* child — his name, interests, and reading level — created in minutes in the app.
2. He can read it on screen with read-aloud and word help, then check what he understood.
3. A parent can **print it**, read it together on paper, or keep reading it in the app any time.
4. Parents see progress and stay in control. It is private and safe — no ads, no chat, no public gallery.

### Shot 1 of 6 — 0:00–0:10 — Hook: bored and nothing that's "his"

Stylized 3D cartoon, warm cozy aesthetic. Late-afternoon window light, soft depth of field, the living room, the plush fox beside the boy.

Direction: First 4 seconds — the boy lies slumped sideways on the sofa, chin on the armrest, staring at the ceiling with a long sigh, while the plush fox lies on its side next to him. His mom sits at the other end of the sofa with a laptop on her knees, screen angled away so only a soft glow lights her face. Last 6 seconds — she smiles, looks over at him, and gently tilts the laptop glow toward his side of the sofa. He turns his head, sits up, and his expression changes from bored to curious.

[overlay: none for this shot]

*(VO added in editor at ~0:05: "Some kids just haven't found *their* story yet.")*

End on the boy's curious face, leaning toward the glow. Match into shot 2.

### Shot 2 of 6 — 0:10–0:20 — Create a story about *him* (the boy and mom, same room)

Same look, lighting, and setting as shot 1. The boy has scooted close to his mom so they share the laptop glow.

Direction: Mom taps the laptop while the boy excitedly counts off ideas on his fingers (space, foxes, baking) and she nods along. He grabs the plush fox, holds it up to the glow, and grins as if saying "he's the hero". A soft golden shimmer gathers and swirls up from the glow, and the boy gasps with delight, hands pressed to his cheeks.

[overlay: a simple card showing a learner profile — a name, a few interest chips (space, foxes, baking), and a reading-level tag — building in beside them, then a "story ready" sparkle, composited in post. No real data.]

*(VO: "Tell it what he loves. It writes a story starring him.")*

End on the boy's delighted gasp, hugging the plush fox. Match into shot 3.

### Shot 3 of 6 — 0:20–0:30 — Read it in the app (read-aloud + word help)

Same sofa, now the boy sits cross-legged with a chunky kid-safe tablet propped on a cushion in front of him (screen angled away from camera, only its glow on his face). The plush fox is tucked under his arm. Mom is beside him, relaxed.

Direction: The boy listens, utterly absorbed, as soft warm light pulses gently in time with a calm narrator voice. His lips move silently along with the words. He points at something in the glow and tilts his head, curious about a new word, then taps it and nods with understanding. Camera drifts slowly in, a gentle push on his face.

[overlay: a story-page graphic with colorful generic illustration shapes and one highlighted word with a small definition bubble, plus a speaker icon pulsing softly for read-aloud, composited in post. No readable story text.]

*(VO: "Read it right in the app. Hear it read aloud. Tap a new word to learn it.")*

End on the boy mouthing the new word and smiling, proud. Match into shot 4.

### Shot 4 of 6 — 0:30–0:40 — The story check + the win

Same sofa, same pose, plush fox on his knee.

Direction: The boy taps a few times toward the angled-away tablet, thinking carefully between taps, a finger on his chin, a small scrunch of the nose, then a quick "I've got it!" nod. Over the last 3 seconds he throws both arms up, the plush fox bounces off his lap, and he catches it, laughing. Mom claps silently and grins. Keep the movement snappy and natural.

[overlay: three short answer-choice bubbles with one gently lighting up green, then a few stars and a small "story complete" badge, composited in post. No numbers or real scores.]

*(VO: "A quick story check shows what he understood — and it feels like a win.")*

End on the boy laughing, holding the plush fox up in triumph.

### Shot 5 of 6 — 0:40–0:50 — Print it, read it together (or keep reading on screen)

Same living room, a little later, the light slightly warmer. The home printer on the shelf hums softly.

Direction: Mom taps the laptop, and across the room the small home printer starts, sliding a few warm cream sheets into its tray. The boy hops up, grabs the pages, and runs back to the sofa. The two sit side by side with the pages on their laps, taking turns pointing along with a finger, the boy reading a line aloud, mom reading the next, both laughing. The plush fox sits on the armrest. For the last 3 seconds, a quick glance back: the tablet glow still waiting on the cushion, showing he can pick the story up there again whenever he likes.

[overlay: a tiny print icon pulsing as the printer starts, then a small "keep reading in the app anytime" tag appearing near the tablet glow, composited in post. The printed sheets have no readable text.]

*(VO: "Print it and read it together on paper — or keep reading it in the app, anytime.")*
