# How To Use Story Sprout

## What Story Sprout Does

Story Sprout helps an adult and child create and read a personalized story together. The adult chooses the learner, grade level, reading skill, story world, adventure, and story length. The child can help choose ideas and then read or listen to the story, learn new words, and answer comprehension questions.

Children do not create their own accounts. A parent, guardian, teacher, or authorized educator manages the account and learner profiles.

## 1. Open The Website

Open:

```text
https://dansprout.com
```

For a new visitor, the site displays the Story Sprout sign-in screen.

You can choose:

- **Sign in** if you already have an account.
- **Create account** if you are a new user.
- **Continue with local demo** only when you want to explore browser-only demo behavior.

The local demo is separate from the authenticated PostgreSQL account. Demo stories are stored in the browser and are not the same as production account data.

## 2. Create An Adult Account

Select **Create account** and complete:

1. Your name.
2. Account type: Parent or guardian, or Teacher.
3. Email address.
4. Password.
5. Confirm password.
6. Adult consent checkbox.

The adult consent confirms that the person creating the account is an adult parent, guardian, or authorized educator and approves learner profiles and AI story generation.

Select **Create secure account**.

After successful registration, Story Sprout signs you in and opens the protected family workspace.

## 3. Sign In

Enter the account email and password, then select **Sign in**.

If you are already signed in, Story Sprout opens the family workspace automatically. Select **Sign out** in the top navigation when you want to end the session.

Use **Forgot password?** if you need to request a password reset. Password reset email delivery must be configured by the service operator in production.

## 4. Add A Learner

The first setup step is to create a learner profile.

Open **Learners** and select **Add a learner**. Enter:

- Learner first name.
- Age range: Ages 3-5, Ages 6-8, or Ages 9-11.
- Interests, such as space, animals, soccer, music, or baking.
- Topics to avoid, if needed.

Save the learner.

Only the signed-in adult account can access that learner profile. The learner does not need an email address or separate login.

## 5. Create A Story Together

Open **Home** and find **Create a story together**.

Complete the story form:

### Learner

Select the learner who will read or listen to the story.

### Grade Level

Choose PreK through Grade 8. The selected grade guides:

- Sentence length.
- Vocabulary difficulty.
- Story structure.
- Question difficulty.
- Reading expectations.

### Reading Skill For This Grade

Choose the reading skill or curriculum objective. The menu shows the strand and standard code. The plain-English objective appears below it.

The curriculum guide explains common abbreviations:

- `RF` = Reading Foundational Skills
- `RL` = Reading Literature
- `RI` = Reading Informational Text
- `L` = Language
- `W` = Writing

The curriculum code is a guide for the skill being practiced. It is not a formal diagnosis or automatic proof of mastery.

### Story World

Choose a story world such as Moonlight, Rainforest, Ocean, Castle, Garden, Sky, Space, or another available option. You can also choose a custom story world.

### Adventure

Describe what should happen in the story. For example:

```text
Finding a map beneath a moonlit bench
```

The child can help invent the adventure.

### Story Length

Choose one of the simple length options:

- **Quick read:** approximately 100-180 words.
- **Standard story:** approximately 180-350 words.
- **Longer adventure:** approximately 350-700 words.

The grade level still controls the reading difficulty. The length choice controls how much story is created.

Select **Generate reading-aligned story**.

## 6. Read The Story

After generation, open the saved story from the story list.

The story reader provides:

- Story pages.
- Page navigation.
- Previous and next controls.
- Read this page.
- Read story.
- Stop narration.
- Text size controls.
- Vocabulary words.
- Story completion control.

An adult and child can read together, or the child can use the read-aloud feature when appropriate.

## 7. Learn Vocabulary

Story Sprout can show selected vocabulary words from the story with child-friendly meanings.

A word can be added to the Word garden by selecting the word action. Parents and children can use the Word garden to review useful story vocabulary.

The story should be reviewed by an adult for suitability and accuracy before use.

## 8. Answer The Questions

Newly generated stories use multiple-choice comprehension questions.

Each question has:

- A question based on the story.
- Two or three answer choices.
- One correct answer.
- No true-or-false format for new stories.

The child should select the best answer using information from the story. Questions become more difficult as the selected grade level increases.

Select **Check answers** when all questions are answered.

## 9. Understand The Score

After checking answers, Story Sprout displays:

- A large score out of 100.
- The number of correct answers.
- Encouragement based on the result.
- A **Try again** button.
- A **Close** button.

For example:

```text
67/100
2 of 3 correct. Keep practicing this skill and try again.
```

The score measures understanding of that story's questions. It is a story-check score, not a complete reading assessment or guaranteed proof of mastery.

A score of 80 or higher can mark the story check as demonstrating the selected skill. This should still be interpreted as evidence from one story activity, not a full educational diagnosis.

## 10. Try Again Or Finish

### Try Again

Select **Try again** to clear the selected choices and answer the questions again.

### Close

Select **Close** to end the question activity and return to the story view or close the story reader.

## 11. Mark A Story Complete

When the child finishes reading, select **Mark completed**.

Completion records that the story was finished. Completion is different from mastery:

- **Completed** means the story was read or finished.
- **Score** means the child answered the story questions.
- **Mastered** means the automatic story check reached the configured threshold for the selected activity.

## 12. Review Saved Stories

Open **Home** or the story library to see saved stories.

Saved story information can include:

- Story title.
- Learner name.
- Story world.
- Grade level.
- Reading domain.
- Curriculum standard code.
- Curriculum objective.
- Creation time.
- Completion status.

Open a saved story to read it again or review its reading information.

## 13. View Progress

The progress area can show information such as:

- Number of learners.
- Stories saved.
- Stories completed.
- Skills marked as mastered by the story-check system.
- Average reviewed assessment score where available.

Progress is intended to help adults notice practice over time. It should not replace a teacher's evaluation or a formal reading assessment.

## 14. Understand The Curriculum Connection

Each generated story uses the selected grade, domain, standard code, and objective during generation.

For example, a parent may select:

```text
Grade 1
Main Idea and Details
1-CMP-1
```

The AI then receives the objective and creates a story and questions intended to practice that skill.

The curriculum code and objective are saved with the story so the adult can see why the story was created and what reading practice it supports.

## 15. Privacy And Data Controls

Open **Privacy & data** from the navigation.

Available controls can include:

- Download my data.
- Cancel subscription.
- Delete account and learner data.

Privacy information explains:

- What account and learner data is stored.
- How AI processing works.
- Which vendors support the service.
- How account deletion works.
- Why adults must manage learner profiles.

Do not enter unnecessary sensitive information about a child. Avoid full names, contact details, medical information, or other private information that is not needed for a story.

## 16. Plans And Billing

Open **Plans & billing** to view available plan options.

The site may offer demo plan selection or paid checkout depending on the production configuration.

Before selecting a paid plan, review:

- Price.
- Trial period.
- Renewal terms.
- Cancellation terms.
- Refund information.

Payment details are handled by the payment provider rather than stored directly by Story Sprout.

## 17. Adult And Child Roles

### Adult

The adult:

- Creates the account.
- Adds learner profiles.
- Chooses grade and reading objectives.
- Reviews stories.
- Supervises use.
- Manages privacy and data.
- Controls plans and billing.

### Child

The child can:

- Suggest interests and adventures.
- Help choose story ideas.
- Read or listen to the story.
- Learn vocabulary.
- Select answers to comprehension questions.
- Try questions again.
- Discuss the story with an adult.

## 18. Recommended Family Routine

A simple session can look like this:

1. Choose a story idea together.
2. Select the learner and reading goal.
3. Generate the story.
4. Read one page at a time.
5. Ask the child what they noticed.
6. Review one or two new words.
7. Complete the multiple-choice questions.
8. Look at the score together.
9. Retry the questions if useful.
10. Mark the story complete.
11. Save the story for another reading session.

## 19. Important Limitations

Story Sprout should be used as a reading-practice tool, not as:

- A replacement for a teacher.
- A formal diagnostic assessment.
- A medical, psychological, or therapeutic service.
- A guaranteed method for improving reading scores.
- A substitute for a school curriculum.

Adults should review generated content and supervise a child's use of the service.
